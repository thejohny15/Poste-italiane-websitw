Share of Electricity Generation in Europe

Percentage share (monthly, stacked)

This figure shows that renewable energy sources have been playing an increasingly important role in Europe’s electricity generation mix over the last few years. Wind and solar in particular account for a growing share of electricity production, reflecting the European Union’s long-term strategy to expand renewable energy capacity and reduce dependence on fossil fuels. Over the same period, the share of gas in electricity generation has remained relatively stable but has slowly declined on average, fluctuating around roughly 17% of total generation.

Although this figure focuses only on electricity production, it illustrates a broader structural trend in Europe’s energy system: the gradual diversification of energy sources and a continued reduction in reliance on coal. Coal’s contribution to electricity generation has steadily decreased as many European countries have closed or scheduled the closure of coal-fired power plants as part of climate and decarbonization policies.

However, geopolitical shocks—such as the current conflict involving Iran—could temporarily alter this trajectory. If the conflict were to significantly disrupt energy markets and push gas prices sharply higher, some European governments have indicated that they may consider temporarily restarting certain coal plants to stabilize electricity supply and limit the economic impact of higher energy prices. Such measures would likely be short-term emergency responses rather than a reversal of Europe’s broader transition toward renewable energy.
